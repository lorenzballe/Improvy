when it contains flat and sharp signs, bullets, em dashes, ellipses or curly
quotes, so degrees are written `b3` and `#11` here even though the app itself
prints them properly.

Nothing here claims a feature the app does not have. In particular there is no
minor-key mode and no interval-recognition ear training, and neither is
mentioned.

## Description

```
Musicians talk in numbers. The flat three. The sharp eleven. The five of the five.

If you still count up from the root every time you hear one, you are doing arithmetic on stage. Improvy closes that gap the only way it closes: a few seconds at a time, hundreds of times.

HOW IT WORKS

A degree appears. You tap the note. That is the whole loop, and it is fast enough to run while the kettle boils.

Every one of the 12 major keys is in here, and the app spells each one the way a musician writes it. In Eb the flat two is Fb, not E. In Db the flat two is E double flat. Most apps quietly get this wrong. Improvy does not, because the moment it does the habit you are building is the wrong one.

THE MODES

Diatonic - the seven notes of the scale. Where everyone starts.

Chromatic - all twelve, with the altered extensions charts actually print: b9, #9, #11, b13.

Note to Number - the other direction. You see a note, you name its degree. Knowing that F is the flat three of D is not the same skill as knowing the flat three of D is F, and you need both.

...Of What? - a note is a given degree. Name the root. This is the one that turns a melody into changes: if that A is the sharp eleven, you are on Eb.

Custom Mode - pick the key, the direction, and the exact degrees. For the six you keep missing.

Pocket Mode - hands free. A voice asks, waits, then says the answer. It runs with the screen off, so it works on a walk, on the train, or in the ten minutes before a gig.

DAILY CHALLENGE

Fifteen chromatic questions against the clock, the same for everyone, once a day. Miss a day and the streak goes. That is the point.

FREE MODE

No score, no timer, no session. A degree, then another when you tap. For warming up, for practising with an instrument in your hands, for teaching someone at a piano.

WHAT YOU GET BACK

Accuracy and speed per key and per degree, so you can see the three that are actually slowing you down instead of guessing.

SETTINGS THAT MATTER

Simple Note Names turns off enharmonic spelling everywhere at once, if you would rather work in the twelve names you already use.

FREE AND PRO

Free gives you Diatonic mode in C, the Daily Challenge, Free Mode and your stats. Enough to know within a week whether this is for you.

Pro unlocks all 12 keys, Chromatic, Note to Number, ...Of What? with extensions, Custom Mode, adaptive difficulty and the per-key analytics.

Pro is a one-time payment. Not a subscription. Buy it once and it stays bought.

No ads. No account. No sign-up. Nothing to cancel.
```
